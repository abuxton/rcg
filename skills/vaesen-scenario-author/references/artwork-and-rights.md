# Artwork and rights

Source: Free League Publishing press page, <https://freeleaguepublishing.com/press/>.
Treat that page and any asset-specific terms as authoritative. Re-read them each
time; they can change.

## Finding artwork

1. Open the press page and look for the game's press pack (for example the
   Vaesen or *Mythic Britain & Ireland* material).
2. Pick an asset whose subject and shape suit the scenario. A wide banner suits
   the full-width opening.
3. Prefer the highest-resolution version offered; save to `assets/scenarios/`.

## Check and retain terms

- Read the terms on the press page and in any pack README or licence file.
- Record in the scenario's working notes: page URL, pack name, asset name,
  artist if credited, date accessed, and the usage terms as quoted.
- Do not crop out credits or edit the art beyond what the terms allow.
- Store the terms alongside the asset or in your project notes so they can be
  shown later.

## Link and credit

Place a credit line beneath the banner, as in the reference scenario:
`Banner artwork supplied from the Free League Publishing press pack.` Where
the artist is named, name them. Link the press page wherever the art appears
(PDF footer and any article). Give the image meaningful alt text.

## When art or permission is unavailable

- Omit the banner; open with the title in the masthead (the layout still works).
- Use plain typography, a rule or an original illustration.
- Never use art from search results, other products or sites without clear permission.
- If the terms are unclear, ask the publisher rather than guessing.

## Keep rights questions separate

Press-pack artwork permission covers the art, as stated by its terms. It does
**not** by itself grant any right to:

- publish game text or rules,
- use setting IP, names or lore, or
- sell or distribute a game-compatible product.

Prompt the author to check these independently against the publisher's current
community-content or licensing terms, and to describe the work as unofficial
fan-made material where that is the case. Marketplace availability does not
confer game-IP rights either. Do not state or imply that a licence exists
unless the author has confirmed it.
