This is a living index. The idea is simple: as the blog accumulates posts, a single page
that organises them by year becomes useful — both for new readers who want to find their
footing and for me as a record of what I have actually managed to write. The
[nine-year retrospective]({{ '/posts/2026/05/04/nine-years-of-red-circle-games.html' | relative_url }})
covers everything from 2017 to the end of 2024. This page picks up from 2025 onwards.

I will update it as new posts appear.

---

## 2025

No posts were published in 2025. That is not a gap I am proud of, but it is the honest
record. Ideas were accumulating in the drafts folder and in the GitHub issues backlog — they
just did not make it out as finished posts that year.

---

## 2026

2026 has been the most productive stretch of writing this blog has seen in several years.
Ten posts across three months, covering AI, game system alternatives, digital publishing,
L(A)RP design, and a few things that resist easy categorisation. In rough order of
publication:

---

### [AI in TTRPGs — Two Years On, What Changed?]({{ '/2026/04/26/ai-developments-revisited.html' | relative_url }})

*26 April 2026 · Tags: TTRPG, AI, LLM, tools*

A proper revisit of the
[2024 post on AI assistants and gaming]({{ '/2024/06/01/AI-prompt-gms.html' | relative_url }}),
written with the benefit of two more years of watching that space move. Covers what I got
right, what changed faster than expected, and a survey of the actual tools — Avrae, AI
dungeon generators, LLM-powered NPCs — that the TTRPG community has been building and using.
The short version: the technology improved dramatically and the ethical questions got harder,
not easier.

### [Nine Years of Red Circle Games — A Retrospective]({{ '/posts/2026/05/04/nine-years-of-red-circle-games.html' | relative_url }})

*4 May 2026 · Tags: retrospective, rcg, TTRPG, LRP, LARP, AI, technology, game-design*

A long-form map of the whole archive from 2017 to the point of writing. Covers the
recurring themes — technology as a tool not the point, minimal viable thinking, aesthetics
and inspiration, safety and inclusivity — and walks through each year's output with links
and commentary. Useful if you are new to the blog and want the full picture, or if you
want to see how nine years of scattered posts actually fit together.

### [Digital RPG Books and Why Humble Bundle Deserves Your Attention]({{ '/posts/2026/05/05/digital-rpg-books-and-humble-bundle.html' | relative_url }})

*5 May 2026 · Tags: TTRPG, digital, humble-bundle, PDF, accessibility, charity*

A look at the digital RPG book ecosystem and why platforms like Humble Bundle have become a
meaningful part of it. Covers how PDF distribution changed the economics and accessibility of
tabletop gaming, what the bundles actually contain, and why buying a bundle is a reasonable
way to fill gaps in your library and support charity at the same time. Practical and
unashamedly enthusiastic about digital hoarding with a hobby veneer.

### [Beyond the Dungeon — The Abundance of Alternatives to D&D]({{ '/posts/2026/05/09/beyond-the-dungeon-ttrpg-alternatives.html' | relative_url }})

*9 May 2026 · Tags: TTRPG, dnd, alternatives, shadowrun, cyberpunk, call-of-cthulhu, blades-in-the-dark, pathfinder, OSR*

A survey of the wide and genuinely excellent landscape of TTRPG alternatives to *Dungeons &
Dragons*, from cyberpunk dystopias to eldritch horror, from the dark streets of Duskvol to
the grim corridors of Warhammer's Old World. Written partly as a response to the OGL debacle
of 2023, which sent a lot of players looking sideways for the first time. The argument is
straightforward: D&D has an enormous gravitational pull on the public idea of what a TTRPG
is, and that has obscured some genuinely excellent games that deserve attention.

### [From Drafts to Issues]({{ '/posts/2026/05/12/drafts-to-issues.html' | relative_url }})

*12 May 2026 · Tags: rpg, l(a)rp, writing, planning*

A short process note on moving ideas out of the `_drafts/` folder and into tracked GitHub
issues. Less a blog post and more a public accountability mechanism: by turning each
lingering draft into an issue with a label and a description, the backlog becomes visible
and manageable rather than a quiet pile of good intentions. Links to the specific issues
opened for Apeiron, Bad Company L(A)RP, GPS player tracking, Strontium Dog L(A)RP, and a
few others.

### [AI Dungeon Masters — But What About AI Players?]({{ '/posts/2026/05/14/ai-dungeon-masters-but-what-about-ai-players.html' | relative_url }})

*14 May 2026 · Tags: TTRPG, AI, LLM, DnD, GameMaster, agents, solo, tools*

The AI GM space is crowded. This post asks the less-examined question: what happens when the
human at the table is the Dungeon Master and the party are all AI agents? Covers the
mechanics of running a solo game with LLM players, the practical experience of doing it,
what it is actually useful for (playtesting, prep, solo play between sessions), and what it
reveals about the social contract of the table that we normally take for granted.

### [Forty Years from the Fallen Gate — A Preface]({{ '/posts/2026/05/18/forty-years-from-the-fallen-gate.html' | relative_url }})

*18 May 2026 · Tags: rpg, ttrpg, larp, l(a)rp, writing, dungeons-and-dragons, retrospective*

A working preface for a collection of short stories drawn from forty years of gaming — the
campaigns, the borrowed worlds, the characters that refused to stay in the notebooks. Starts
with a memory of the first *Dungeons & Dragons* session I ever played, at a table in a
schoolroom in 1985, and attempts to explain why those fragments of play matter enough to
collect and write up. Unpolished by design: the voice is deliberate, the roughness is part
of it.

### [Apeiron: Odyssey After the Flood]({{ '/posts/2026/06/01/apeiron.html' | relative_url }})

*1 June 2026 · Tags: l(a)rp, odyssey, mythic-modern, game-design, event-design*

A reworked draft for a modern mythic L(A)RP. The premise: Atlantis fell two thousand years
ago, but the descendants of heroes, old cults and bloodlines still move through modern cities.
The games happen in car parks, freight yards and underpasses. The aesthetic draws on 1980s
*Highlander* (ancestral weapons, private wars in a modern skyline) and 1990s *Fight Club*
(hidden gatherings, coded identities, factions that recruit through shared anger). Combat-led,
with a strong visual frame and a social structure players can grasp quickly.

### [Review: Dragonbane as a Lighter Replacement for D&D]({{ '/posts/2026/06/14/dragonbane-free-league-review.html' | relative_url }})

*14 June 2026 · Tags: dragonbane, free-league, ttrpg, dungeons-and-dragons, fantasy, review*

A proper review of *Dragonbane* from Free League, prompted by giving it only a paragraph in
the "Beyond the Dungeon" post. Covers what *Dragonbane* does differently, why its lighter
mechanics suit groups who have spent years in *D&D 5e* and want danger and character without
the weight of the full 5e rules stack, and what you lose in the exchange. Written by someone
with a lot of history with *D&D* who also has real affection for cleaner, more direct systems.

### [Fantasy and Science Fiction Short Stories from Campaign Notes]({{ '/posts/2026/06/17/fantasy-and-science-fiction-short-stories-from-campaign-notes.html' | relative_url }})

*17 June 2026 · Tags: rpg, ttrpg, l(a)rp, writing, fantasy, science-fiction, self-publishing, campaign-design*

On the practice of turning campaign notes into short fiction — and why that pile of
unfinished notes should be treated as source material rather than failure. Covers the
mechanics of how a two-page scene or a witness account can seed an entire campaign, how
self-publishing works as a testing ground for ideas, and why opening a game with short story
hooks changes what players bring to the first session.
